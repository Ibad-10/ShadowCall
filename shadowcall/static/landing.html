The idea

Big AI models are clever but costly. Small ones are cheap but untested.

Every phone call to a voice agent costs money, because a big AI model does all the thinking. A small model could do much of it for a fraction of the price. The hard part is knowing when it's safe to trust it. ShadowCall answers that with evidence, one job at a time.

Today

  • The big model answers every call.
  • Every call costs the full price.
  • Nobody checks its answers against the rules.

With ShadowCall

  • A small model learns by watching, quietly.
  • A rule checker marks both models on every call.
  • Each job switches only when the small one has proved itself. Then that job costs much less.
How it works

Six steps on every call.

Click a step to read about it, or let it play. A call is split into four jobs: understand the caller, check the rules, fill the booking form, and write the summary. Each job earns its switch on its own.

Safety

It says no until the evidence says yes.

Agreeing with the big model isn't enough. The small model has to be right by the practice's own rules.

  • A fair test. The score comes from test calls the small model never learned from.
  • Emergencies come first. Missing one emergency call, even once, blocks a switch.
  • New rules, new test. Change the booking rules and every job goes back to the big model until it passes again.
  • People have the last word. Mistakes the big model can't fix wait for a person to approve.
  • Still watched after a switch. The big model spot-checks 1 in 5 switched calls. If scores drop, the job switches back by itself.

Questions

Plain answers.

Who answers the caller while the small model learns?

The big model, exactly as today. The small model's answers are only compared, never heard, until its job switches.

What is the "rule checker"?

Plain code built from the practice's booking rules, such as opening hours, prices, and “new patients need a check-up first”. It isn't another AI, so it can't be talked round. It marks each answer right or wrong.

What happens when the big model is wrong?

It's shown the checker's note and asked again. If the new answer is right, it's saved as a learning example. If not, it waits for a person. So the small model learns from right answers only.

What is Mubit's part?

Mubit is the gateway both models go through. It collects the checked examples and, once a job has 25 of them, it can train the small model on them.

Are the numbers on this page real?

They come live from this server. In mock mode both models are simulated, which tests the pipeline but isn't a result. The page says which mode it's in.

Can a job go back to the big model?

Yes. In one click from the console, on its own if spot-check scores drop, and always when the rules change.
